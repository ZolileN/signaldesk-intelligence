The fundamental principle is:
Different sources observe reality. SignalDesk determines what those observations mean together.

2. What VectaNews Should Actually Do
VectaNews should specialise in turning the enormous news ecosystem into structured observations.
Its job is not merely:
"Find news articles."
Its job is:
"Detect, structure, classify, cluster, and track developments reported across the news ecosystem."
The data flow should be:
NEWS SOURCES
     ↓
Discovery
     ↓
Collection
     ↓
Deduplication
     ↓
Article Extraction
     ↓
Classification
     ↓
Entity Extraction
     ↓
Claim Extraction
     ↓
Event Detection
     ↓
Event Clustering
     ↓
SignalDesk

So VectaNews becomes the News Intelligence Ingestion Layer.

3. The Difference Between VectaNews and SignalDesk
This distinction is extremely important.
VectaNews asks:
What are the media sources reporting?
It produces:
Article
     ↓
Claim
     ↓
Entity
     ↓
Event

Example:
Article:
"Residents protest against mining company's proposed expansion."

Entities:
- Mining Company X
- Community Y
- Municipality Z

Event:
Community protest against mining expansion.


SignalDesk asks:
What is happening in the real world, based on all available evidence?
It takes:
Article 1
Article 2
Radio discussion
Government statement
Social activity
Historical events

and asks:
Are these connected?

Then:
Event 1
       ┐
Event 2 ───► SITUATION
Event 3
       ┘

This is the distinction:
VectaNews detects events. SignalDesk understands situations.

5. The Critical Layer: Event Clustering
This is where VectaNews becomes strategically valuable.
Imagine 50 articles:
Article 1:
"Residents protest mining project."

Article 2:
"Police monitor community gathering."

Article 3:
"Mining company delays operations."

Article 4:
"Mayor calls for emergency meeting."

Article 5:
"Community leaders threaten further action."

A traditional news aggregator sees:
5 articles

VectaNews should see:
5 observations
        ↓
1 developing event

SignalDesk should then see:
DEVELOPING SITUATION:
Growing community opposition to Mining Project X.

This is a major conceptual shift.

6. VectaNews and Radio Should Produce the Same Data Type
This is essential for the architecture.
The radio pipeline produces:
Broadcast
     ↓
Transcript
     ↓
Claim
     ↓
Event

VectaNews produces:
Article
     ↓
Text
     ↓
Claim
     ↓
Event

Both should eventually produce the same canonical object:
{
  "event_id": "evt_123",
  "event_type": "community_protest",
  "location": "Location X",
  "entities": [
    "Community Y",
    "Company Z",
    "Municipality A"
  ],
  "time": "2026-07-22T10:00:00",
  "claims": [],
  "sources": [],
  "confidence": 0.82
}

This is the architectural key.
The upstream sources can be completely different.
But once they reach the Event Layer, they become interoperable.

9. How VectaNews Answers the Eight Questions
The SignalDesk core promise is:
What is happening?
Is it getting worse or better?
Where is it happening?
Who is involved?
What is driving it?
What happens next?
How am I exposed?
What should I do?
Let's map VectaNews into this.

1. What is happening?
VectaNews contributes:
Articles
     ↓
Events
     ↓
Event Clusters

Example:
A developing protest movement is forming around a mining project.
The system knows because:
5 articles
+
2 radio discussions
+
government statement

are all connected.

2. Is it getting worse or better?
This requires time-series analysis.
VectaNews tracks:
Event Frequency
Mention Volume
Source Diversity
Geographic Spread
Actor Escalation
Language Intensity

Example:
Monday:
2 articles

Tuesday:
7 articles
3 radio discussions

Wednesday:
18 articles
12 radio discussions
Government statement

SignalDesk might conclude:
Trajectory: ESCALATING
Confidence: HIGH

The important point:
A situation is not worsening merely because more articles are published.
We need multiple signals:
More actors involved
+
More locations involved
+
More severe actions
+
More institutional response

That is stronger.

3. Where is it happening?
VectaNews extracts:
Country
Province
City
Municipality
Community
Facility
Road
Mine
Border

The event can then be geo-coded.
Event
    ↓
Location
    ↓
Map
    ↓
Spatial Spread

This is especially powerful for:
Municipalities.
Mining companies.
Investors.
Government.
NGOs.
Infrastructure companies.

4. Who is involved?
The entity layer identifies:
People
Companies
Political parties
Government departments
Municipalities
Communities
NGOs
Institutions

Then SignalDesk can build:
Actor A
    │
    ├── supports
    ├── opposes
    ├── funds
    ├── regulates
    ├── owns
    └── influences

This becomes an Actor Relationship Graph.

5. What is driving it?
This is where SignalDesk must go beyond article summaries.
The system should distinguish:
Trigger
Cause
Grievance
Catalyst
Underlying Driver

Example:
Immediate Trigger:
Company announces expansion.

Underlying Driver:
Long-running land dispute.

Community Grievance:
Employment promises not fulfilled.

Political Catalyst:
Local election approaching.

VectaNews supplies the evidence.
SignalDesk performs the synthesis.

10. What Happens Next?
This is one of the most difficult and valuable parts.
The system can analyse:
Current Situation
        ↓
Historical Similar Situations
        ↓
Current Trajectory
        ↓
Actor Behaviour
        ↓
Likely Next Developments

Example:
Community complaint
        ↓
Public meeting
        ↓
Threat of protest
        ↓
Actual protest
        ↓
Road blockade
        ↓
Police intervention

If SignalDesk detects the early stages, it can identify:
The situation is moving toward potential operational disruption.
This does not mean pretending to predict the future with certainty.
It means:
Identify plausible next developments based on observed trajectory and comparable patterns.

11. How Am I Exposed?
This is where SignalDesk becomes a commercial product rather than merely an intelligence platform.
A situation by itself is not enough.
The customer needs to define:
MY ORGANISATION
       │
       ├── Assets
       ├── Operations
       ├── Employees
       ├── Supply Chain
       ├── Investments
       ├── Locations
       └── Strategic Interests

Then:
SITUATION
    ↓
Exposure Engine
    ↓
Customer Profile

Example:
Situation:
Community protest near Mine X.

Customer:
Investment Fund Y.

Exposure:
Fund owns 12% of Mine X's parent company.

Impact:
Potential operational disruption.

Risk:
Medium → High.


This is a critical architectural distinction:
SignalDesk needs a world model and a customer model.
The world model says:
What is happening?

The customer model says:
Why should I care?


12. What Should I Do?
The final layer should not be:
"Here is a summary."
It should be:
Situation
    ↓
Impact
    ↓
Recommended Response

For example:
Recommended actions:

1. Monitor the community meeting scheduled for Friday.
2. Confirm operational contingency plans.
3. Review stakeholder engagement.
4. Prepare communications response.
5. Escalate to regional management.

The recommendation should be:
Evidence-Based
Situation-Specific
Role-Specific

A mining company and a journalist should not receive the same recommendation.

15. The Most Important Product Insight
VectaNews should not compete with news aggregators on:
"We have more articles."
It should compete on:
"We understand how individual reports connect to developing situations."
And SignalDesk should not compete with dashboards on:
"We show you more data."
It should compete on:
"We tell you what is happening, whether it is changing, how it affects you, and what you should do."
That is the product hierarchy:
┌───────────────────────────────────────┐
│          DECISION INTELLIGENCE         │
│                                       │
│       What should I do?               │
├───────────────────────────────────────┤
│             EXPOSURE                  │
│                                       │
│       How am I affected?              │
├───────────────────────────────────────┤
│            TRAJECTORY                 │
│                                       │
│       What happens next?              │
├───────────────────────────────────────┤
│            SITUATION                  │
│                                       │
│       What is actually happening?     │
├───────────────────────────────────────┤
│              EVENTS                   │
│                                       │
│       What happened?                  │
├───────────────────────────────────────┤
│              SIGNALS                  │
│                                       │
│       Articles │ Radio │ Social       │
└───────────────────────────────────────┘

VectaNews sits at the bottom and middle of the intelligence stack. SignalDesk sits at the top.
The strategic moat is not simply the VectaNews technology or the radio listener.
The moat is the historical event and situation graph that develops over time:
Sources
   ↓
Signals
   ↓
Events
   ↓
Situations
   ↓
Trajectories
   ↓
Outcomes

Eventually SignalDesk can learn:
“When situations with this combination of actors, grievances, locations and escalation patterns develop, these are the outcomes that typically follow.”
That is where VectaNews, Qisimah and SignalDesk stop being three separate ideas and become one coherent intelligence infrastructure platform.
