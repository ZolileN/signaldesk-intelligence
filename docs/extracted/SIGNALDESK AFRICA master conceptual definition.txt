1. Executive Summary
SignalDesk Africa is an African-first situation intelligence infrastructure platform designed to help organisations understand what is happening in the world around them, how situations are developing, how they may be exposed, and what they should do next.
The platform converts fragmented, unstructured and rapidly changing information into structured, evidence-backed decision intelligence.
The central promise is:
The Eight Questions
What is happening?
Is it getting worse or better?
Where is it happening?
Who is involved?
What is driving it?
What happens next?
How am I exposed?
What should I do?
SignalDesk does not fundamentally compete as:
A news aggregator.
A media-monitoring platform.
A social listening tool.
A radio transcription service.
A dashboard of information.
Its core product is:
A system that observes the world, identifies developing events, connects them into situations, determines trajectory and drivers, maps exposure, and produces decision intelligence.
The architecture is designed to ingest information from multiple sources, including:
News media.
Online publications.
Radio broadcasts.
Social media.
Government documents.
Corporate disclosures.
Public datasets.
Regulatory information.
Historical events.
Customer-provided internal data.
These sources are converted into a unified intelligence model.

4. The Canonical Intelligence Model
The entire platform requires a common intelligence data model.
4.1 Source
A source is where information originates.
Examples:
News website.
Radio station.
Government department.
Company.
Social media account.
Official document.
Research institution.
Source
├── source_id
├── name
├── source_type
├── country
├── region
├── language
├── ownership
├── historical_reliability
├── political_context
└── metadata

4.2 Observation
An observation is a piece of information captured from a source.
Examples:
An article.
A radio broadcast.
A social post.
A government announcement.
Observation
├── observation_id
├── source_id
├── timestamp
├── raw_content
├── language
├── location
├── content_type
└── provenance

4.3 Claim
A claim is a proposition contained within an observation.
Example:
“Residents are planning to block the road.”
The system should record:
Claim
├── claim_id
├── text
├── claimant
├── source
├── timestamp
├── evidence
├── contradicting_evidence
├── verification_status
└── confidence
Claims must be distinguished from facts.
The system must support:
FACT
CLAIM
ALLEGATION
RUMOUR
OPINION
NARRATIVE
PREDICTION

An entity is a person, organisation, location, asset, institution, policy or other identifiable object.

├── relationships

12. Source Reliability and Confidence
SignalDesk must never treat all sources equally.
Source reliability should consider:
Historical accuracy.
Correction history.
Original reporting.
Firsthand access.
Geographic proximity.
Topic expertise.
Independence.
Potential bias.
Corroboration.
A small community radio station may have firsthand information that a large international publication does not.
Therefore reliability must be contextual.
The confidence engine should consider:
Source Reliability
+
Evidence Quality
+
Independent Corroboration
+
Contradictory Evidence
+
Temporal Consistency
Output:
Confidence:
High / Medium / Low

Verification:
Confirmed / Partially Verified / Contested / Unverified

26. The Core Product Philosophy
SignalDesk should follow five principles.
1. Evidence Before Intelligence
Every conclusion should be traceable.
2. Context Over Volume
The platform should reduce information overload, not increase it.
3. Situations Over Mentions
A mention is not necessarily an event.
An event is not necessarily a situation.
4. Exposure Over Generality
The most valuable intelligence is specific to the customer.
5. Action Over Information
The final output should help someone make a decision.
