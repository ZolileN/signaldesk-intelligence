1. Executive Summary
SignalDesk is an intelligence infrastructure platform that continuously monitors information from news, radio, government sources, public documents, social platforms, and other data sources to identify meaningful changes in the real world.
The platform transforms fragmented information into structured, continuously evolving intelligence.
SignalDesk is designed to answer eight questions:
What is happening?
Is it getting worse or better?
Where is it happening?
Who is involved?
What is driving it?
What happens next?
How am I exposed?
What should I do?
The core product is not an AI news summariser.
It is not simply a media-monitoring platform.
It is not merely a social-listening tool.
The core product is:
A continuously updated model of important real-world situations and their implications for an organisation.
SignalDesk converts:
Information
    ↓
Signals
    ↓
Events
    ↓
Patterns
    ↓
Situations
    ↓
Trajectory
    ↓
Scenarios
    ↓
Exposure
    ↓
Recommended Action


2. Product Vision
Vision
To become the intelligence infrastructure through which organisations understand what is changing in Africa and the world, how those changes may affect them, and what they should do next.
Mission
SignalDesk exists to reduce the gap between:
Something happening in the world
and:
An organisation understanding what it means for them.
The platform should enable organisations to move from reactive intelligence gathering to continuous situational awareness.

3. The Core Problem
Organisations today have access to enormous amounts of information.
The problem is not lack of information.
The problem is the inability to efficiently convert information into decision-relevant intelligence.
A typical organisation may use:
Google Search
News alerts
RSS feeds
Social media
Radio
WhatsApp groups
Government websites
Consultants
Analysts
Excel
Email
Internal reports

The result is fragmentation.
An analyst may spend hours answering:
What happened?
Is this related to something else?
Is this getting worse?
Which locations are affected?
Who is involved?
What is the underlying cause?
Could this spread?
Does it affect our operations?
What should management do?
The information exists.
The problem is that the organisation must manually reconstruct the situation.
SignalDesk automates and continuously maintains that reconstruction.

4. Product Thesis
The central thesis is:
The valuable intelligence unit is not the article, post, broadcast or document. The valuable intelligence unit is the evolving situation.
Therefore:
Source ≠ Signal
Signal ≠ Event
Event ≠ Situation
Situation ≠ Exposure
Exposure ≠ Risk

SignalDesk should transform:
Thousands of disconnected information items

into:
A small number of evolving situations

that users can understand and act upon.

A source may be:

Less reliable for political claims.

Weak on analysis.

9.2 Content
Content is the raw information collected.
Examples:
Article.
Broadcast.
Transcript.
Government document.
Social media post.
Court document.
Press release.
The original content must be retained for traceability.

9.3 Signal
A signal is a potentially meaningful piece of information extracted from content.
One article may generate several signals.
Example:
Article
 ├── Government announces policy
 ├── Community threatens protest
 ├── Company suspends operations
 └── Political party responds

Each signal becomes a structured intelligence object.

9.4 Event
An event represents an observable occurrence.
The event model:
WHO
DID WHAT
TO WHAT
WHERE
WHEN
UNDER WHAT CONDITIONS

Example:

Residents

Action:
Blocked

Object:
Major road

Location:
Municipality X

Time:
22 July 2026


9.5 Claim
A claim is an assertion that requires evidence assessment.
Example:
"Foreign nationals are taking all the jobs."
The platform should record:

Where it appeared.
How widely it spread.
Supporting evidence.
Contradictory evidence.
Evidence status.
The platform should distinguish:
Observed
Reported
Supported
Disputed
Contradicted
Unverified
Unknown

It should avoid presenting every claim as simple TRUE/FALSE.

10. The Situation
The Situation is the core SignalDesk object.
Definition:
A Situation is a meaningful, evolving real-world phenomenon involving connected events, actors, claims, locations, drivers and potential consequences.
Examples:
Growing opposition to a mining project.
Anti-immigration mobilisation.
Political instability.
Emerging energy supply disruption.
Regulatory change affecting an industry.
A misinformation campaign.
A community conflict.
A geopolitical crisis.
A situation is not a single article.
It is an evolving model.

The platform should detect situations through multiple methods.
12.1 Event accumulation

12.2 Anomaly detection
The system identifies unusual activity.
Example:
Normal:
5 mentions per week

Current:
500 mentions in 48 hours

Potential signal:
Emerging situation.

12.3 Entity activity spikes
An organisation, person, project or location suddenly receives abnormal attention.

12.4 Geographic clustering
Multiple related events occur within a geographic area.

12.5 Actor network expansion
A situation becomes more complex as new actors become involved.
Example:
Community
   ↓
Project

Then:

Community
   ├── Political party
   ├── Union
   ├── Media
   └── Government

The expansion of the network can indicate escalation.

13. Situation Matching
Every new event should be assessed against existing situations.
The matching algorithm should consider:
Semantic similarity
+
Entity overlap
+
Location overlap
+
Time proximity
+
Event type
+
Causal relationship

Example:
New event:
Community protest against Solar Project Alpha

Existing situation:
Growing opposition to Solar Project Alpha

If the match score is sufficiently high, the event updates the existing situation.

23. Exposure versus Risk
SignalDesk should distinguish:
Exposure
You are connected to the situation.
Risk
There is a probability of a negative outcome.
Example:
Exposure:
High

Risk:
Medium

Because:
The organisation is geographically close.
But the situation is currently peaceful.
Operational controls are strong.
The initial product should focus primarily on exposure and impact pathways rather than pretending to calculate perfect risk probabilities.
